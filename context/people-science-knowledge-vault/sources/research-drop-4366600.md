## Research Drop: Understanding Voluntary Turnover Through Employee Sentiment

`research-drop-4366600` | research-drop | reviewed

**Priority:** New catalog; before relevant original-vault resources.

**Source:** https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftvivablog/research-drop-understanding-voluntary-turnover-through-employee-sentiment/4366600

January 2025 public article and two figures reviewed. Retrospective June 2023 exit comparison across 33 customers; item-use analysis excludes customized wording. Intention is not behavior and a group exit-rate association is not a validated individual prediction. The source's predictive terminology is not extended to the user's data.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete published article/documentation body. Also reviewed OCR of 2 public figures; numeric claims cross-checked against written text where available. Linked courses and other resources are separate assets, not automatically reviewed. No claim of complete linked-recording coverage.

**Study-family links:** glint-standard-item-utilization-2022-2024

1. **Retention matters during transformation** (commentary) - Talent continuity is framed as relevant to AI skills and changing work policies.
   Topics: retention, change-management. Evidence: `L18`, "key talent".
2. **Ask about intentions directly** (finding) - Intent to Stay captures planned continuity and is the strongest subsequent-turnover correlate in the cited taxonomy analysis.
   Topics: retention, employee-listening. Evidence: `L6`, "strongest correlate".
3. **Intentions are not a full behavioral proxy** (framework) - The literature discussion explicitly warns against assuming identical conditions govern considering departure and actually leaving.
   Topics: methods, retention. Evidence: `L45`, "proxy sentiment".
4. **Observed 90-day exit-rate contrast** (finding) - The source reports a tenfold subsequent-exit comparison between unfavorable and favorable responders in a separate cross-customer analysis.
   Topics: retention, methods. Evidence: `L58`, "10x".
5. **Low and uneven item utilization** (finding) - Only about a quarter of customers ask the item, with substantial industry differences.
   Topics: survey-design, retention. Evidence: `L64`, "5% to 38%".
6. **Measure retention alongside engagement** (recommendation) - Use Intent to Stay in addition to eSat when retention is an explicit organizational goal.
   Topics: engagement, survey-design. Evidence: `L70`, "strong focus on retention".
7. **Compare scores in context** (recommendation) - Use trends, department comparisons, and suitable benchmarks rather than interpreting an isolated score.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-interpretation. Evidence: `L74`, "departmental differences".
8. **Connect intentions to experiences** (recommendation) - Explore relevant demographics and relationships with other survey items, such as fairness, without treating them as established causes.
   Topics: survey-interpretation, feedback. Evidence: `L77`, "Fair Evaluation".
9. **Trust affects candid reporting** (recommendation) - Confidentiality concerns can suppress honest intentions; explain protections and follow through transparently.
   Topics: psychological-safety, privacy-confidentiality. Evidence: `L81`, "confidentiality".
10. **Improve experience, not only retention numbers** (recommendation) - The end goal is a better employee experience and commitment, not simply higher scores or perfect elimination of turnover.
   Topics: action-taking, retention. Evidence: `L82`, "end goal".
