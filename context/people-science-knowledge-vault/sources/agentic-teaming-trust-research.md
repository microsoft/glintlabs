## Research Readout - Agentic Teaming & Trust Research

`agentic-teaming-trust-research` | catalog | reviewed

**Priority:** Original vault; retained tier `top5`.

**Source:** https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/s/gxcuf89792/attachments/gxcuf89792/MicrosoftVivaBlog/1027.2/2/2025%20Agentic%20Teaming%20%26%20Trust%20Research%20Report%20-%20Chapter%204.pdf

Full 11-page published Chapter 4 only, not the entire multi-chapter report. July 2025 self-report panel: n=1,800 overall, n=819 leaders, n=1,520 agent users in team analyses. Reported mediation/regression is observational, not proof of causal team ROI; no recommendation to reproduce advanced causal modeling.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete extracted text of the public 11-page PDF. Linked courses and other resources are separate assets, not automatically reviewed. No claim of complete linked-recording coverage.

**Study-family links:** agentic-teaming-trust-july-2025

1. **Expected competitive and team potential** (finding) - Respondents anticipate that agents can expand team capabilities and confer competitive advantage; these are expectations, not measured financial returns.
   Topics: ai-agents, ai-value. Evidence: `p3`, "competitive advantage".
2. **Three human-agent operating phases** (framework) - The chapter distinguishes human-with-assistant, human-agent teams, and human-led agent-operated work.
   Topics: ai-agents, work-design. Evidence: `p4`, "Human-agent teams".
3. **Talent strategy remains immature** (finding) - Only 16% of surveyed leaders report an established employee-upskilling strategy for autonomous agents.
   Topics: ai-skills, leadership. Evidence: `p5`, "16%".
4. **Team readiness beyond individual readiness** (framework) - Shared task allocation, trust, goals, skills, and openness define readiness to integrate agents as a team.
   Topics: ai-adoption, ai-agents, role-clarity. Evidence: `p6`, "team-level needs".
5. **Teammate versus personal-tool orientation** (finding) - Higher team readiness is associated with treating agents as teammates rather than only individual tools.
   Topics: ai-agents, work-design. Evidence: `p6`, "individual tools".
6. **Readiness, teaming, and perceived value** (finding) - The source's statistical model links readiness and teaming processes with perceived team value; its modeled variance is not a causal ROI estimate.
   Topics: ai-value, methods. Evidence: `p7`, "45%".
7. **Knowledge sharing and goal alignment** (finding) - Agent-ready respondents report better knowledge sharing, goal alignment, and creative problem-solving than low/neutral-ready respondents.
   Topics: ai-value, work-design. Evidence: `p8`, "goal alignment".
8. **Team output quality and efficiency** (finding) - Readiness groups differ in perceived quality and efficiency benefits; these are survey outcomes, not audited productivity gains.
   Topics: ai-value, work-design. Evidence: `p8`, "quality".
9. **Psychological safety and agent adoption** (finding) - Psychologically safe respondents show higher readiness, value, and frequent agent use; direction of influence is not established.
   Topics: psychological-safety, ai-adoption. Evidence: `p9`, "1.4x".
10. **Upskilling, process change, and safe experimentation** (recommendation) - The chapter recommends training, peer learning, team-process preparation, and an environment where experimentation can fail safely.
   Topics: ai-skills, change-management. Evidence: `p10`, "Cultivate".
