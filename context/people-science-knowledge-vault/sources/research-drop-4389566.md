## Research Drop: Investing in Training Opportunities to Close the AI Skills Gap

`research-drop-4389566` | research-drop | reviewed

**Priority:** New catalog; before relevant original-vault resources.

**Source:** https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftvivablog/research-drop-investing-in-training-opportunities-to-close-the-ai-skills-gap/4389566

April 2025 public article and two figures reviewed. Primary analysis: April 2024 AI-readiness panel n=1,800; other named external reports supply contextual statistics. Training confidence is self-report, not a randomized training intervention or demonstrated return on investment.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete published article/documentation body. Also reviewed OCR of 2 public figures; numeric claims cross-checked against written text where available. Linked courses and other resources are separate assets, not automatically reviewed. No claim of complete linked-recording coverage.

**Study-family links:** ai-readiness-april-2024

1. **AI literacy needs systematic investment** (recommendation) - Contextual studies describe skill gaps and reskilling demands; the article argues for enterprise-wide learning.
   Topics: ai-skills, ai-strategy. Evidence: `L16`, "enterprise-wide AI training".
2. **Training confidence and frequent use** (finding) - Daily AI use is much more common among respondents who feel adequately trained.
   Topics: ai-adoption, ai-skills. Evidence: `L38`, "43%".
3. **Training and perceived future relevance** (finding) - Trained respondents more often believe they have irreplaceable skills, suggesting a confidence-related support need.
   Topics: ai-skills, wellbeing, growth-development. Evidence: `L42`, "irreplaceable skills".
4. **Training and realized individual benefits** (finding) - Adequately trained respondents are 1.9 times as likely to report RIVA outcomes; this is an association.
   Topics: ai-value, ai-skills. Evidence: `L44`, "1.9x".
5. **Accessibility and capacity are separate barriers** (framework) - Having a course available is different from being able to invest working time and attention in it.
   Topics: learning-enablement, work-design. Evidence: `L51`, "two main obstacles".
6. **Tool access and training confidence** (finding) - High company-tool access coincides with greater training confidence, with department-level access gaps.
   Topics: inclusion, ai-skills. Evidence: `L56`, "80%".
7. **Burnout and learning readiness** (finding) - Training confidence falls from 83% among those without burnout indicators to 55% among those with three or more.
   Topics: wellbeing, ai-skills. Evidence: `L64`, "55%".
8. **Make learning routes visible and equitable** (recommendation) - Communicate who can access opportunities and find departments excluded from development.
   Topics: inclusion, learning-enablement. Evidence: `L74`, "clear company communications".
9. **Scale peer-led and adaptable training** (recommendation) - Champion networks and adaptable training kits can support role- and region-specific continuous learning.
   Topics: ai-skills, learning-enablement. Evidence: `L75`, "training-in-a box".
10. **Protect learning time and model it** (recommendation) - Managers can reserve time, make development a team goal, and demonstrate that learning is safe and valued.
   Topics: leadership, work-design. Evidence: `L76`, "monthly calendar hold".
