# Glint Labs definition brief

> **Working draft for team discussion — not an approved strategy**

## Why this document exists

Glint Labs now has tangible work—a 2026 Annual Report, an Analyze Survey skill,
early site concepts, and a growing set of research and tool ideas—but no shared
definition of what Glint Labs is, who it is for, or what belongs within it.

This brief gathers the strongest signals from recent team conversations and
turns them into hypotheses the team can challenge. Its purpose is to create
alignment, not to present decisions as settled.

## How to review this brief

Comment using one of these prompts:

- **Agree** — this reflects the direction you believe the team has discussed.
- **Challenge** — this framing is wrong, incomplete, or too narrow.
- **Evidence** — add a customer signal, research finding, strategy decision, or
  example that should shape the definition.
- **Gap** — identify a question, audience, risk, or constraint that is missing.

## Core contributors

The living definition should continuously include these perspectives:

- People Science and research authority.
- HR customer or audience insight.
- Glint Labs strategy or product leadership.
- Content design and editorial.
- UX or product design.
- Engineering and publishing feasibility.
- Responsible AI.

One named editor or facilitator should maintain the document and synthesize
comments. Glint Labs strategy or product leadership and People Science should
jointly approve any statement that becomes durable direction.

## Collaboration approach

The primary working surface should be a shared Word document in the team's
normal SharePoint or Teams space. Reviewers should use comments to preserve
questions, evidence, and disagreement rather than silently rewriting one
another's positions.

This is a living definition document rather than a timed review round. It
should continuously:

- Strengthen the evidence behind the brief.
- Identify important gaps and constraints.
- Make disagreements visible.
- Preserve working hypotheses without presenting them as decisions.
- Record approved direction only after joint strategy/product and People
  Science agreement.

The document does not need to reach a permanently final state before the team
can make progress. Durable decisions and shared vocabulary should be
synchronized back into the repository when they are approved.

There is no recurring review cadence or formal close to the definition phase.
Contributors can add evidence, questions, and challenges continuously, and the
editor should keep the current state of the conversation understandable.

## What prior conversations appear to establish

These are recurring signals, not final decisions:

1. **Glint Labs combines knowledge and application.** Conversations consistently
   pair People Science research and publications with skills, prompts,
   templates, analyses, frameworks, and other reusable resources.
2. **The intended value extends beyond reading.** The work is expected to help
   people make decisions, evaluate programs, run analyses, apply methods, or
   bring People Science into an existing workflow.
3. **External organizational leaders matter.** External HR strategy leaders,
   HR program leaders, and employee-listening leaders are repeatedly named as
   important audiences.
4. **Different work may serve different specialist audiences.** Annual reports
   can serve a broad leadership and practitioner audience, while analytical
   skills may serve hands-on analysts or advanced survey users.
5. **Internal reuse is also important.** Microsoft product, design, and People
   Science teams may use Glint Labs work as reusable intellectual material or
   as a source for future product experiences.
6. **Some content may be consumed by AI systems as well as people.** Prior
   discussions include AI-ready research, skills, assistant integration, and
   structured knowledge that can be retrieved or executed.
7. **Glint Labs is not simply product marketing.** The recurring concept is a
   People Science knowledge and practice destination, even though its exact
   relationship to Microsoft Viva Glint still needs an agreed expression.

## Current working hypotheses

These statements are starting points for discussion. They should change when
the team supplies stronger evidence or a clearer ambition.

### Possible purpose

> Glint Labs helps people apply credible People Science to consequential
> workplace decisions.

Questions:

- Is “apply” the core promise, or should the purpose begin with discovery,
  experimentation, publication, or community?
- Is “workplace decisions” specific enough to guide the work?
- What value can Glint Labs uniquely provide that product documentation,
  academic publishing, consulting, and general thought leadership do not?

### Possible primary audience

> An external leader accountable for an organizational people program who
> needs evidence, methods, or practical resources to make and implement a
> decision.

Possible examples include HR strategy leaders, employee-listening leaders,
people-experience program owners, and AI-transformation or change leaders.

Questions:

- Is there one primary audience, or does Glint Labs intentionally serve several
  audiences equally?
- Which role has the clearest unmet need that Glint Labs can address?
- When an executive, program owner, analyst, manager, and AI assistant need
  different levels of detail, whose needs should determine the default
  experience?

### Possible content families

The current work suggests at least three possible families:

- **Research** — reports, findings, articles, and evidence syntheses.
- **Tools** — skills, prompts, templates, analysis workflows, and reusable
  assets.
- **Applied practice** — methods, frameworks, examples, and guidance for using
  People Science in context.

Questions:

- Are these the right boundaries, or are they artifacts of the work currently
  available?
- Where would evaluations, benchmarks, datasets, prototypes, or case studies
  belong?
- Should visitors navigate by content type, workplace question, topic, role,
  or a combination?

## Productive tensions to resolve

The definition should make an explicit choice—or an explicit balance—across
these tensions:

| Tension | Questions for the team |
| --- | --- |
| Authority vs. experimentation | Does “Labs” mean reviewed People Science guidance, early work shared openly, or a clearly labeled mix? |
| Broad reach vs. focused usefulness | Should the site welcome any interested reader while designing primarily for one professional audience? |
| Research vs. action | Is the main value publishing trusted evidence, helping people act, or connecting the two? |
| Human readers vs. AI consumers | Is AI-readable content a core requirement, a distribution strategy, or a future possibility? |
| Viva Glint affiliation vs. distinct identity | How prominently should Microsoft Viva Glint appear, and what should readers infer from that relationship? |
| Durable library vs. current showcase | Is Glint Labs building a lasting knowledge base or highlighting a small set of current initiatives? |
| Openness vs. responsible use | What research, methods, tools, or data-related workflows should not be published without additional safeguards? |

## Decisions the team needs to make

The definition phase should conclude with shared answers to these questions:

1. Why does Glint Labs need to exist?
2. Who should receive the most value from it first?
3. What should that audience be able to understand or do because Glint Labs
   exists?
4. What types of work belong in Glint Labs?
5. What explicitly does not belong?
6. What does “Labs” promise about quality, maturity, and experimentation?
7. What is the relationship between Glint Labs, Microsoft Viva Glint, product
   experiences, and product documentation?
8. How should research, tools, and methods show their evidence, limitations,
   ownership, and freshness?
9. What would meaningful success look like after six and twelve months?
10. Who decides what is published, updated, retired, or promoted into another
    Microsoft experience?

## Existing v1 material to use as concrete examples

The team can test every proposed definition against the work already planned:

- **2026 Annual Report** — a substantial People Science research publication.
- **Analyze Survey** — a reusable analytical skill with privacy and responsible
  use implications.
- **Future destinations** — research, tools, collaborations, subscription, and
  other areas currently represented as under construction.

A useful definition should explain why the Annual Report and Analyze Survey
belong together without becoming so broad that nearly any HR-related work could
be called Glint Labs.

## Proposed output of the definition conversation

The goal is not a long strategy deck. Alignment should eventually be captured
in a concise set of statements:

- A one-sentence purpose.
- A prioritized audience model.
- A clear value promise.
- A small set of content boundaries.
- A position on authority and experimentation.
- A statement of relationship to Microsoft Viva Glint.
- Initial measures of useful impact.
- Named ownership for publishing and maintenance.

Those statements can then guide the site structure, homepage copy, content
templates, visual design, and v1 roadmap.
