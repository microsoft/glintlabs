Centered SVD exploration

Singular value decomposition (SVD) summarizes variation across numeric survey items as a smaller set of components. Use explained variance and item weights to explore shared response patterns; this is not a validated measurement model or a predictive leaderboard.

How to action these results

Read the highest-weighted items together to form a working hypothesis about each component. Check content coverage and stability before naming a dimension or shortening a survey; a high weight is not a causal priority for intervention.

Item weights by component

Bar length and darker shading show absolute SVD unit weights on the same 0-1 scale across components, not factor loadings. Shading does not indicate validated strength or significance. Labels retain signs; positive and negative do not mean good and bad. Rows are ordered by Component 1 magnitude; item labels are formatted IDs.

      Model comparison
      

      Compares predictive models on the same development folds. Use the agreed metric and baseline to judge whether a more complex model adds useful predictive accuracy.
      

      How to action these results
      Prefer the simplest candidate that meets your agreed improvement criterion over the baseline. Freeze selection and request untouched-holdout evaluation before making a final performance claim; development scores alone do not establish practical usefulness.
      

      Development validation selects candidates. Fold variation is not a confidence interval. Blank holdout values mean not evaluated, not zero.
      

      

      Selected diagnostics
      

      Shows performance measures for the model you select, with residuals, calibration, or feature importance when those diagnostics are available. Use training-versus-validation differences to spot possible overfitting.
      

      How to action these results
      Use gaps, residual patterns, or calibration issues to propose one bounded next experiment. Review feature meaning and timing, then obtain explicit instruction before changing inputs, settings, or thresholds; predictive importance does not identify causes.
      

      Choosing a view does not retrain a model. Permutation importance is not causal influence; correlated predictors may share importance.
