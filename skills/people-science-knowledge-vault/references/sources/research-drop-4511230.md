## Research Drop: Tracking the Rise of Perceived AI Value at Work

`research-drop-4511230` | research-drop | reviewed

**Priority:** New catalog; before relevant original-vault resources.

**Source:** https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftvivablog/research-drop-tracking-the-rise-of-perceived-ai-value-at-work/4511230

April 2026 article comparing three independent samples of AI-using managers and ICs: April 2024 global n=910, July 2025 global n=811, November 2025 US-only n=610, total n=2,331. No repeated participants; geography, usage mix, composition, small subgroups, and missing departmental comparisons limit trends to directional snapshots, not within-person growth. RIVA is the mean of six item favorability percentages, not a transformed mean or audited financial ROI. The May 2026 Drop analyzes the same samples.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete freshly retrieved public article body read, including substantive published method/item appendices where present. Figures, chart pixels, linked assets, audio, private decks, and notes were not reviewed in this pass. Current themes are grounded in body prose, not chart alt text. Prior figure review is historical only and is recorded in the audit; it is not refreshed coverage.

**Study-family links:** ai-readiness-april-2024, agentic-teaming-trust-july-2025, ai-outcomes-november-2025

1. **Measure perceived work benefits beyond adoption** (framework) - RIVA asks whether AI improves individual work quality, speed, complexity, productivity, decisions, and stress. Rising use or favorable perceived benefits alone do not demonstrate enterprise transformation or audited financial returns.
   Topics: ai-value, survey-design. Evidence: `L2`, "adoption alone doesn't tell us".
2. **RIVA favorability averages six item percentages** (method) - The publication calculates RIVA by averaging the favorable-response percentages across six items, with favorable meaning Agree or Strongly Agree. It is not an audited productivity metric or a rescaled mean Likert score.
   Topics: ai-value, survey-design, methods. Evidence: `L56`, "averaging the favorability percentage of the six individual RIVA items".
3. **Separate samples show a directional rise, not personal growth** (finding) - The article reports a nine-point favorability rise across three samples of AI-using managers/ICs. Different participants and a US-only latest sample mean this cannot demonstrate changes within employees or a causal effect of maturation.
   Topics: ai-value, methods. Evidence: `L7`, "not repeated samples of employees".
4. **Usage and perceived value are intertwined** (finding) - More frequent users reported greater value, with the author explicitly describing a likely reinforcing, not causal, relationship. People who value AI may use it more, while practice may also deepen perceived value.
   Topics: ai-adoption, ai-value, methods. Evidence: `L25`, "likely reinforcing, not causal".
5. **A changing usage mix can move the aggregate** (finding) - Value was comparatively stable within usage-frequency groups while more respondents appeared in higher-frequency categories. Aggregate change can therefore reflect composition, not universal improvement within a fixed usage group.
   Topics: ai-adoption, ai-value, survey-interpretation. Evidence: `L26`, "perceived value for each usage group remained relatively stable".
6. **Managers and ICs show different value patterns** (finding) - Managers generally reported more value, the gap narrowed in the middle snapshot, and IC gains appeared earlier with little later movement. Coordination-heavy work and team-dependent next steps are plausible interpretations, not established reasons for those differences.
   Topics: manager-effectiveness, ai-value, work-design. Evidence: `L32`, "Individual contributors, by contrast, saw their gains earlier".
7. **Midsize respondents report higher value than larger groups** (finding) - Employees in organizations of 1,000–4,999 reported more value than large or enterprise groups in these snapshots. Faster integration and fewer governance silos are proposed mechanisms, not isolated effects of organization size.
   Topics: ai-value, work-design, methods. Evidence: `L35`, "midsize organizations (1,000 to 4,999 employees)".
8. **Industry value pathways are directional and context-specific** (finding) - The prose describes differing trajectories, including healthcare momentum and financial/professional-services friction. Small industry groups and different regulatory/workflow contexts argue against declaring a universal winning sector or importing one playbook unchanged.
   Topics: ai-value, ai-governance, work-design. Evidence: `L43`, "different paths to value".
9. **IT remains high while HR rises in the later snapshot** (finding) - IT consistently reported high value, while HR approached it after a later increase. Proximity to transformation decisions and reduced administrative burden are the author's explanations, not evidence that owning implementation causes higher RIVA.
   Topics: hr-it, ai-value. Evidence: `L47`, "a big jump for HR employees".
10. **Engineering reports a distinct growth pattern** (finding) - The article describes continuing R&D/Engineering value increases across snapshots. Its cited developer-productivity experiment is external evidence, not an objective productivity result of this panel comparison.
   Topics: ai-value, ai-skills, work-design. Evidence: `L50`, "research & development (R&D)/engineering".
11. **Experienced value need not remove professional uncertainty** (commentary) - The author notes that finding useful AI benefits can coexist with anxiety about role identity and relevance. Framing AI as a skill amplifier and enabling job crafting are proposed supports, not tested anxiety-reduction interventions.
   Topics: change-management, growth-development, wellbeing. Evidence: `L51`, "Rising value perception and professional uncertainty can".
12. **Distribute value through workflows and guardrails** (recommendation) - Embed AI in real work, provide trustworthy boundaries, and support evolving team practices so benefits do not concentrate only among employees closest to the transformation. These are recommendations based on uneven reported experiences.
   Topics: ai-strategy, ai-value, inclusion, ai-governance. Evidence: `L53`, "benefits of AI risk concentrating".

## Shared research context

### ai-readiness-april-2024

April 2024 global AI readiness research, n=1,800 overall; profile, function, frontline, and training analyses use different subsets. Repeated articles/decks and the later n=910 snapshot are related analyses, not independent replications.

### agentic-teaming-trust-july-2025

July 2025 global panel, n=1,800 overall (819 leaders, 520 managers, 461 ICs). Chapter 4 and monthly drops use differing subsets/constructs; later n=811 managers/ICs form one of the repeated-publication snapshots. Multiple publications are not multiple independent studies.

### ai-outcomes-november-2025

US-only November 2025 sample of 610 AI-using managers/ICs, reused by the April and May 2026 articles. It is not a repeated-person follow-up of the two earlier global samples.
