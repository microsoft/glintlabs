## Research Readout - Agentic Teaming & Trust Research

`agentic-teaming-trust-research` | catalog | reviewed

**Priority:** Original vault; retained tier `top5`.

**Source:** https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/s/gxcuf89792/attachments/gxcuf89792/MicrosoftVivaBlog/1027.2/2/2025%20Agentic%20Teaming%20%26%20Trust%20Research%20Report%20-%20Chapter%204.pdf

Complete 11-page published Chapter 4 only, not the entire multi-chapter report. July 2025 self-report online panel: n=1,800 overall, n=819 leaders, n=1,520 agent users for team analyses. Nine markets, organizations with 1,000+ employees; figure footnotes specify English-speaking respondents. Regression/mediation and group contrasts are observational; RIVA/RTVA are perceived individual/team benefits, not audited ROI. No recommendation to reproduce advanced causal modeling.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete current cached public PDF page text reviewed. Public PDF figures visually inspected on pages 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9. Other image-only details are not certified. Linked publications, courses, and other companion assets are not newly reviewed unless explicitly stated.

**Study-family links:** agentic-teaming-trust-july-2025

1. **Agent enthusiasm concerns potential rather than achieved performance** (finding) - In July 2025, 75% believed teams embracing agents would gain competitive advantage, 71% saw potential to reach otherwise-unattainable goals, and 70% were excited to manage agents. These are expectations, not realized financial benefits.
   Topics: ai-agents, ai-strategy, ai-value. Evidence: `p3`, "competitive".
2. **Human-agent work is framed in three operating phases** (framework) - The chapter distinguishes human-with-assistant, human-agent teams, and human-led/agent-operated work. This is an organizing model, not proof every organization follows the same sequence.
   Topics: ai-agents, work-design, role-clarity. Evidence: `p4`, "Human-agent teams".
3. **Talent strategy is often still being planned or implemented** (finding) - Only 16% of 819 leaders reported an established employee-upskilling strategy for autonomous agents; planning and implementing were the largest displayed categories. This measures leaders' reported preparation, not independently audited readiness.
   Topics: ai-skills, leadership, ai-agents. Evidence: `p5`, "only 16%".
4. **Readiness includes team coordination, not only individual skill** (framework) - Team agent readiness covers task allocation between humans and agents, trust for appropriate tasks, shared outcome goals, skills, and openness to workflow integration.
   Topics: ai-agents, role-clarity, ai-governance. Evidence: `p6`, "team-level needs".
5. **Ready teams more often treat agents as teammates** (finding) - Employees reporting higher team readiness were more likely to describe agents as teammates rather than personal tools; the chapter does not establish that assigning a teammate label causes better work.
   Topics: ai-agents, work-design. Evidence: `p6`, "Agents as Teammates".
6. **Mediation model is observational and measures perceived team value** (method) - Among 1,520 agent users with teams, a model controlling job level and use frequency explained 45% of variance in perceived team value; agent-driven teaming accounted for 46% of the modeled total relationship. This is not a causal pathway estimate or audited financial ROI.
   Topics: ai-agents, ai-value, methods. Evidence: `p7`, "Mediation analysis".
7. **Readiness relates to collaboration and perceived output benefits** (finding) - The inspected chart contrasts high-readiness teams with low/neutral ones: knowledge sharing, goal alignment, and creative problem-solving differ by 31 percentage points, while perceived quality and efficiency differ by 29 points. These are self-report group differences from shared respondents, not independent intervention outcomes.
   Topics: ai-agents, ai-value, connection, methods. Evidence: `p8`, "+31pp".
8. **Psychological safety is associated with readiness, use, and value** (finding) - Psychologically safe employees were 1.4 times as likely to be frequent agent users, and the inspected chart shows higher individual/team readiness and perceived value. Safety is associated with these outcomes; the survey does not show that a safety intervention caused them.
   Topics: psychological-safety, ai-adoption, ai-agents. Evidence: `p9`, "1.4x".
9. **Support skills, process redesign, and safe experimentation** (recommendation) - The chapter recommends training, resources and peer learning; planning for changed team processes; and an environment where experimenting and potentially failing with agents is safe. These are practical recommendations rather than tested program effects.
   Topics: ai-skills, psychological-safety, work-design. Evidence: `p10`, "Support employees on their journey".
10. **Panel and analysis subsets bound generalizability** (method) - The underlying online panel included 1,800 full-time respondents in nine markets at organizations with 1,000+ employees, collected June 11–July 7, 2025. Figure footnotes specify English-speaking respondents; leader and agent-user analyses use narrower subsets. Aggregates may not reflect every organization or industry.
   Topics: methods, ai-agents. Evidence: `p11`, "between June 11, 2025 and July 7, 2025".
