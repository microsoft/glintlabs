## Reimagining People Success in the age of AI

`people-success` | catalog | reviewed

**Priority:** Original vault; retained tier `high`.

**Source:** https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftvivablog/it%E2%80%99s-a-brave-new-work-world-reimagining-people-success-in-the-age-of-ai/4428208

June/July 2025 synthesis: headline scope is over 6.8M responses across 235+ companies during 2022–2025; the methods separately describe engagement across 230 customers and scores in 2022, 2023, and 2025. The six-element model originates in 350M responses from 2020–2022. Customer correlations and observed voluntary-exit comparisons are not causal AI effects or individual predictions; AI applications include recommendations and secondary-source commentary.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete current cached public article body reviewed; comments, page chrome, and link directories excluded from substantive evidence. Embedded figures were not re-reviewed; claims are restricted to readable prose/page text, not image-only details. Linked publications, courses, and other companion assets are not newly reviewed unless explicitly stated.

1. **Six enduring People Success needs** (framework) - The framework organizes employee experience around purpose, growth, clarity, empowerment, connection, and wellbeing; it is a lens for listening and action, not six new AI intervention effects.
   Topics: people-science, engagement. Evidence: `L8`, "Purpose, Growth, Clarity, Empowerment, Connection and Wellbeing".
2. **Meaningful work and observed voluntary exits** (finding) - In the customer analysis, meaningful work was the highest-ranked voluntary-attrition correlate since 2022; favorable respondents were reported as 3.8 times less likely to leave. This is an observed comparison, not a validated individual forecast.
   Topics: meaningful-work, retention. Evidence: `L16`, "3.8x less likely to leave".
3. **Learning and career opportunities** (finding) - Opportunities to learn and grow and good career opportunities were strongly associated with engagement outcomes. The reported learning/growth voluntary-exit comparison was 2.8 times lower among favorable respondents; career and growth are related but not identical items.
   Topics: growth-development, engagement, retention. Evidence: `L21`, "Opportunities to Learn and Grow".
4. **Protected, role-relevant AI learning** (recommendation) - Create time and psychological safety to experiment, with accessible resources, expert coaching, and manager guidance; prioritize targeted learning relevant to current work rather than simply adding broad training.
   Topics: ai-skills, learning-enablement, psychological-safety. Evidence: `L22`, "time to experiment and empathy".
5. **Clarity about roles and priorities** (finding) - Role expectations, priorities, and useful feedback were associated with observed voluntary exits. Employees who knew what to focus on were reported as 2.7 times less likely to leave; managers should clarify priorities during change rather than treat AI-generated task lists as sufficient.
   Topics: role-clarity, retention, change-management. Evidence: `L30`, "2.7x less likely to leave".
6. **Performance feedback remains a human manager role** (finding) - Performance feedback was the strongest reported correlate of satisfaction with one's manager over three years. The article recommends retaining human coaching and developing managers' feedback skill even when AI can assist.
   Topics: feedback, manager-effectiveness. Evidence: `L28`, "the #1 driver of satisfaction".
7. **Resources and permission to improve work** (finding) - Resources and continuous process improvement correlated strongly with engagement; encouragement to find better ways of working had become especially strongly associated with satisfaction, recommendation, and belonging.
   Topics: empowerment, engagement, work-design. Evidence: `L34`, "Encouraged to Try New Ways".
8. **Use AI capacity for meaningful rather than more menial work** (recommendation) - The author recommends directing time freed by AI toward higher-value, meaningful contributions and using job-specific scenarios and prompts to make empowerment practical. These are proposed applications, not effects tested in the customer attrition analysis.
   Topics: meaningful-work, ai-value, work-design. Evidence: `L18`, "higher value work".
9. **Belonging as a retention-related experience** (finding) - Belonging consistently ranked among the top five correlates of stated retention and observed voluntary attrition over three years. Connection includes collaboration, inclusion, feeling valued, and being accepted as oneself.
   Topics: connection, inclusion, retention. Evidence: `L42`, "one of the top five drivers".
10. **Accessible AI support for inclusion** (recommendation) - The article proposes transcription, summarization, and communication assistance as ways AI may support different communication needs and neurodiverse colleagues. These use cases are recommendations, not new inclusion-effect estimates from the customer study.
   Topics: inclusion, connection, ai-value. Evidence: `L43`, "supporting neurodiverse employees".
11. **Care, support, and balance gained salience** (finding) - Feeling cared for, supported, and able to balance work and life became more strongly associated with engagement and voluntary attrition after 2023. The article reports a 14% uptick in work-life balance's attrition-related importance since 2024, not a 14% change in exit rates.
   Topics: wellbeing, retention. Evidence: `L48`, "largest uptick (14%)".
12. **Communicate AI plans and invite concerns** (recommendation) - Explain why AI is being implemented, its intended benefits, and employee-data protections; build psychological safety so employees can raise concerns. The article's proposed burnout analytics and assistants are not validated individual-risk models in this study.
   Topics: ai-governance, change-management, psychological-safety, wellbeing. Evidence: `L51`, "how employee data will be protected".
13. **Engagement analysis uses averaged customer correlations** (method) - The methods describe Pearson correlations between 25 critical-needs items and six engagement outcomes, averaged across customers within each reported year. Trend emphasis selects the top third of correlation changes; reported drivers are associations, not causal levers.
   Topics: methods, engagement, survey-interpretation. Evidence: `L60`, "correlation strength of the linear relationship".
14. **Attrition comparisons have a narrower eligible sample** (method) - Voluntary-exit analyses required at least 15 customers with at least 80 voluntary terminations between surveys, comparing favorable and unfavorable responses and averaging item multipliers at customer level. This is narrower than the headline millions of survey responses.
   Topics: methods, retention. Evidence: `L62`, "at least 80 voluntary terminations".
