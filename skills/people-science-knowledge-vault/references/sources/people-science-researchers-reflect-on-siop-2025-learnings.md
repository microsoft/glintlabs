## People Science Researchers Reflect on SIOP 2025 Learnings

`people-science-researchers-reflect-on-siop-2025-learnings` | catalog | reviewed

**Priority:** Original vault; retained tier `low`.

**Source:** https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftvivablog/people-science-researchers-reflect-on-siop-2025-learnings/4403530

People Science practitioners' reflections on SIOP 2025, not a single new empirical study. Treat forecasts and conference consensus as commentary.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete current cached public article body reviewed; comments, page chrome, and link directories excluded from substantive evidence. Embedded figures were not re-reviewed; claims are restricted to readable prose/page text, not image-only details. Linked publications, courses, and other companion assets are not newly reviewed unless explicitly stated.

1. **People Scientists as humans in the loop** (commentary) - Conference reflections advocate active human involvement in AI-enabled hiring, career development, and repeatable work, with guardrails against bias and unreliable decisions. This is practitioner commentary rather than an evaluation of those interventions.
   Topics: people-science, ai-governance, work-design. Evidence: `L5`, "proper guardrails".
2. **AI coaching may broaden access** (hypothesis) - Practitioners anticipate AI scaling coaching, career planning, feedback, and training suggestions to employees who currently lack access, including frontline workers; these are expected opportunities, not demonstrated effect sizes.
   Topics: feedback, growth-development, manager-effectiveness. Evidence: `L8`, "employees who do not normally have access".
3. **Empathy in moments of vulnerability remains interpersonal** (commentary) - The conference consensus described human support as particularly important when employees face emotional difficulty; anticipated AI coaching benefits do not establish that people will feel equally supported by a machine.
   Topics: connection, manager-effectiveness, wellbeing. Evidence: `L9`, "moments of great need, vulnerability, and struggle".
4. **Passive data is valuable only when it informs action** (recommendation) - Expanding passive and non-sentiment collection should help leaders and managers make decisions, not become an end in itself. Return useful insights to leaders, managers, and individual contributors instead of keeping them within the data-owning function.
   Topics: employee-listening, action-taking. Evidence: `L12`, "shared back to the people".
5. **Psychological safety supports learning through change** (commentary) - The reflections recommend safe spaces for honest feedback, questions, vulnerability in training, and experimentation with AI and agents; the conference narrative is not a causal trial.
   Topics: psychological-safety, change-management, learning-enablement. Evidence: `L15`, "speaking up, asking questions, and being authentic".
6. **Reuse established adoption frameworks** (recommendation) - Technology Acceptance Model and social-learning principles are offered as familiar scaffolds for AI-adoption strategy rather than assuming every change problem requires a new framework.
   Topics: ai-adoption, people-science. Evidence: `L19`, "Technology Acceptance Model".
7. **Start with small everyday AI experiments** (recommendation) - Try a few workflow use cases, test and iterate, and use broad initial experimentation to discover where deeper investment is worthwhile.
   Topics: ai-adoption, action-taking. Evidence: `L20`, "start small, and start now".
8. **Build cross-disciplinary People Science capability** (recommendation) - People Scientists are encouraged to understand AI systems and work with IT, product, and design to develop ethical, scalable, human-centered solutions.
   Topics: people-science, hr-it, ai-skills. Evidence: `L21`, "collaborating more closely with IT, product and design".
