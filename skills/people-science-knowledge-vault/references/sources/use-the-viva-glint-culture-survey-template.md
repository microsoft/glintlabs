## Use the Viva Glint Culture Survey template

1. **Culture measures observable group behavior** (documentation) - The Culture Survey asks about collective behaviors rather than individual feelings, reflecting culture as something reinforced by group interactions. It is designed to compare intended culture with employee-experienced culture and guide organizational priorities.
   Topics: survey-design, change-management. Evidence: `L22`, "Glint focuses on measuring observable group behaviors that impact important performance outcomes. This approach is the most valid way of measuring org".
2. **Transformation baseline and full-workforce reach** (documentation) - Use the program to establish a cultural baseline for transformation, leadership/business change or merger/acquisition integration. The guide recommends including all employees for insight and awareness, not restricting it to senior leaders who receive results.
   Topics: employee-listening, change-management. Evidence: `L26`, "This program is for organizations interested in gaining a baseline understanding of their current culture to support transformation. Transformation ca".
3. **Recurring multilingual template** (documentation) - Culture is available as a recurring survey template in every language supported by Glint. The page describes research-validated measurement and executive-level insight; that description is not evidence of local business improvement.
   Topics: glint-admin, survey-design. Evidence: `L20`, "Glint offers a Culture Survey template as part of its library of recurring engagement surveys. The Glint Culture Survey measures the facets of culture".
4. **Collaboration and purpose behaviors** (documentation) - Core items cover cross-group collaboration, speaking up without fear, customer-aware decisions and mission-aligned decisions. The Future Success item asks whether the company has the culture needed for future success.
   Topics: connection, psychological-safety, meaningful-work, survey-design. Evidence: `L47`, "People at [Company Name] collaborate well across groups to get things done.".
5. **Humanity and integrity behaviors** (documentation) - Core culture items ask about genuine care for others' wellbeing, belonging, open/honest communication, and fair/objective decisions.
   Topics: wellbeing, inclusion, survey-design. Evidence: `L59`, "People at [Company Name] take a genuine interest in each other's wellbeing.".
6. **Alignment and execution behaviors** (documentation) - Items measure goal alignment with company strategy, balance between short-term demands and long-term direction, reliable delivery of commitments and timely decisions.
   Topics: role-clarity, work-design, survey-design. Evidence: `L62`, "People at [Company Name] align their goals with the goals and strategy of the company.".
7. **Learning and adaptability behaviors** (documentation) - Items ask whether people learn from mistakes, seek feedback, find better ways of working and adapt to change. These complete the template's eight named cultural dimensions rather than constituting separate empirical studies.
   Topics: growth-development, change-management, survey-design. Evidence: `L74`, "People at [Company Name] take the time to learn from mistakes.".
8. **Open-text strengths and improvement** (documentation) - The template includes words describing cultural improvement opportunities and strengths. Narrative Intelligence synthesizes open-ended feedback; culture benchmarks are included automatically in the global benchmark suite.
   Topics: survey-interpretation, benchmarks. Evidence: `L98`, "What are some words to describe the aspects of your company's culture that can be improved upon?".
9. **Avoid combining full culture and engagement surveys** (documentation) - The guide discourages combining complete culture and engagement surveys because of length and competing core drivers. Culture items can still be integrated into existing agile programs, and onboarding/exit programs can measure culture at lifecycle moments; optional sentiments include safety, innovation, trust and inclusiveness.
   Topics: survey-design, employee-listening. Evidence: `L160`, "​​​Combining culture and engagement surveys isn't recommended. Length and competing core drivers are often hard for participants to manage. Instead, h".
10. **Reinforce and revisit the desired culture** (documentation) - Define and model collective values, align hiring/onboarding/development/performance/engagement/exit experiences, and ask about culture annually to check experienced against desired culture. Repetition and consistency are recommended to build trust.
   Topics: change-management, action-taking. Evidence: `L168`, "Align your desired culture with all aspects of the employee experience.  Reinforce culture across the employee lifecycle, including hiring, onboarding".
