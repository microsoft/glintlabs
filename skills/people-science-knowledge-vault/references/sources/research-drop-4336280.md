## Research Drop:  Diving into AI Transformation Profiles to Understand Unique Needs Across Employee Personas

`research-drop-4336280` | research-drop | reviewed

**Priority:** New catalog; before relevant original-vault resources.

**Source:** https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftvivablog/research-drop-diving-into-ai-transformation-profiles-to-understand-unique-needs-/4336280

November 2024 publication from the April 2024 AI-readiness study, shared with the readiness eBook and profiles deck rather than independent replication. Latent profiles combine engagement, prior change experience, and AI sentiment. The baseline recorded a profile-analysis subset of n=1,389; the current prose does not restate that denominator. RIVA comparisons include AI users only, with small skeptic/pessimist subsets, and measure perceived individual benefits, not audited ROI.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete freshly retrieved public article body read, including substantive published method/item appendices where present. Figures, chart pixels, linked assets, audio, private decks, and notes were not reviewed in this pass. Current themes are grounded in body prose, not chart alt text. Prior figure review is historical only and is recorded in the audit; it is not refreshed coverage.

**Study-family links:** ai-readiness-april-2024

1. **Five profiles combine engagement, change, and AI sentiment** (method) - Latent profile analysis grouped respondents into Multipliers, Advocates, Persuadables, AI Skeptics, and Change Pessimists using engagement, prior change experiences, and AI sentiment. Change experience covers vision, communication, and competence.
   Topics: change-management, ai-adoption, engagement, methods. Evidence: `L6`, "latent profile analysis (LPA)".
2. **Profiles are sample-specific, not a workforce census** (finding) - More than 70% of this study fell into Multiplier or Advocate profiles. The author cautions that local culture and unmeasured experiences such as leadership support or job-security concerns may yield different profiles; the proportions are not universal.
   Topics: ai-adoption, change-management, methods. Evidence: `L16`, "profiles are specific to the study sample".
3. **Use frequency differs across profiles** (finding) - Multipliers were most likely to use AI frequently, with progressively lower typical use across Advocates and Persuadables; nonuse was most common among Skeptics and Change Pessimists. These are concurrent profile associations, not effects of a persona-targeted intervention.
   Topics: ai-adoption, change-management. Evidence: `L30`, "Usage frequency follows a consistent downward pattern".
4. **Measure experienced value as well as use** (finding) - RIVA captures recognized individual work benefits rather than activity alone. Users in the Multiplier and Advocate groups reported the highest value, followed by Persuadables; usage counts do not fully describe a transformation experience.
   Topics: ai-value, survey-design. Evidence: `L34`, "not just that people are using AI at work".
5. **User-only comparisons exclude many hesitant employees** (method) - Only 25% of Skeptics and 43% of Change Pessimists used AI and entered the value comparison. Their small selected user subsets must not represent all employees in those profiles; among these users, pessimists reported higher RIVA than skeptics.
   Topics: ai-value, methods. Evidence: `L35`, "Only a small subset of AI Skeptics".
6. **Task-speed benefits can be easier to recognize** (recommendation) - Less enthusiastic profiles more often recognized faster task completion than less tangible benefits such as reduced stress or better decisions. Role-relevant scenarios are recommended to make broader benefits understandable, not as a guaranteed route to financial return.
   Topics: ai-value, learning-enablement. Evidence: `L36`, "when the value becomes less tangible".
7. **AI skepticism and change fatigue need different support** (finding) - Nonuse can reflect doubts about the technology or overwhelm with organizational change. User-only value patterns also differ: skeptics more often cited simplifying complexity, while pessimists more often cited productivity and decisions; avoid treating all hesitation alike.
   Topics: change-management, ai-value. Evidence: `L37`, "their reasons for hesitation and the value they see in AI are not the same".
8. **Change conditions rather than assigning fixed identities** (recommendation) - The author treats profiles as changeable as environments and experiences evolve. Tailor support through sound change practices instead of using a current profile as an enduring employee label; this analysis does not track actual individual profile transitions.
   Topics: change-management, inclusion. Evidence: `L40`, "not static".
9. **Include every profile in designing the transformation** (recommendation) - Champions can energize adoption while hesitant employees can improve accessible training. Use clear communication and continuous listening so engagement and previous change experience, not only enthusiasm for AI, inform an inclusive rollout.
   Topics: employee-listening, inclusion, change-management. Evidence: `L41`, "all types of profiles can be an asset".
