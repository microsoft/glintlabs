## Propel action-taking through conversations with Viva Glint

`propel-action-taking` | catalog | reviewed

**Priority:** Original vault; retained tier `high`.

**Source:** https://adoption.microsoft.com/files/viva/glint/Propel-action-taking-through-conversations-with-Microsoft-Viva-Glint.pdf

Complete 15-page May 2023 guide blending customer observations, behavioral-science rationale, and practical advice. Reported engagement differences are not randomized causal effects.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete current cached public PDF page text reviewed. Embedded figures were not re-reviewed; claims are restricted to readable prose/page text, not image-only details. Linked publications, courses, and other companion assets are not newly reviewed unless explicitly stated.

1. **Visible follow-through sustains listening credibility** (finding) - The guide links belief that action will follow feedback with engagement and reports favorable changes where managers used action planning. These customer observations motivate follow-through but do not establish randomized causal effects.
   Topics: action-taking, engagement, methods. Evidence: `p2`, "7x more likely".
2. **Distribute ownership instead of overloading HR or managers** (recommendation) - Delayed manager access signals that feedback belongs to HR or executives, while expecting managers to fix every item is also overwhelming. Release useful results promptly and clarify the part leaders, managers, and employees each own.
   Topics: action-taking, leadership, manager-effectiveness. Evidence: `p3`, "Ownership of action is unclear".
3. **Enable managers to handle feedback without defensiveness** (recommendation) - Managers need training and practical support; feedback can feel personal, outside their control, or intimidating, especially without psychological safety. Treat these barriers as enablement needs rather than assuming data alone produces action.
   Topics: manager-effectiveness, feedback, psychological-safety. Evidence: `p4`, "Feedback can feel hard to act on".
4. **Build an agile system across people, technology, and strategy** (framework) - The framework combines shared skills/mindsets, accessible meaningful insights, and a strategy integrated into business priorities. Its historical product features illustrate support, not a current guarantee of every named capability.
   Topics: action-taking, people-science, employee-listening. Evidence: `p5`, "3 key components".
5. **Replace elaborate plans with one manageable shift** (recommendation) - Shorten feedback-to-action time, focus on one impactful change, iterate, and keep a simple recurring check-in. Long plans and annual-only action cycles can lose momentum before work begins.
   Topics: action-taking, work-design. Evidence: `p6`, "one impactful".
6. **Conversations create shared understanding and accountability** (recommendation) - Regular conversations let teams explore feedback, agree what to solve, share responsibility, and correct course. They remain possible when no fresh survey exists or a team lacks enough responses for its own report, without bypassing reporting confidentiality.
   Topics: feedback, action-taking, privacy-confidentiality. Evidence: `p7`, "a big enough team".
7. **ACT structures a repeatable feedback conversation** (framework) - Acknowledge where we are, Collaborate on where we want to go, and Take one step forward is a conversation scaffold intended to make connection and improvement habitual, not an intervention-effect estimate.
   Topics: action-taking, feedback. Evidence: `p8`, "Conversation Guide".
8. **Discuss hypotheses with the team and recognize progress** (recommendation) - Bring results and tentative interpretations to an open discussion rather than deciding the problem and solution alone. Start with positive progress stories and invite employee participation in choosing a focused priority.
   Topics: psychological-safety, feedback, action-taking. Evidence: `p9`, "bring their hypotheses".
9. **Stop as well as start behaviors** (recommendation) - Use start/stop/continue discussion to uncover behaviors that help or hinder progress; adding more tasks without removing anything can dilute focus and make dissent harder.
   Topics: action-taking, work-design, empowerment. Evidence: `p10`, "start, stop,".
10. **Make individual commitments and schedule the next check-in** (recommendation) - Shared ownership needs individual commitments so responsibility does not diffuse. Agree when to revisit progress, share lessons and obstacles, and treat adjustment as learning rather than failure.
   Topics: action-taking, feedback. Evidence: `p10`, "individual commitments".
11. **Integrate action into current work and priorities** (recommendation) - Use regular meetings instead of separate action-planning meetings, and select an issue the team can influence that matters to its next three to six months of work. Systemic issues may require leadership ownership rather than a local promise.
   Topics: action-taking, leadership, role-clarity. Evidence: `p12`, "key business activities".
12. **Managers coach, facilitate, unblock, and connect** (framework) - Managers do not fix engagement in isolation: they model constructive behavior, include different voices, escalate organizational constraints, and sustain priority-aligned conversations and connections across teams.
   Topics: manager-effectiveness, action-taking, connection. Evidence: `p13`, "Roadblock Remover".
13. **Demonstrate impact rather than compliance activity** (recommendation) - Senior leaders should model felt accountability and teams should tell concrete stories of changed work or business impact, rather than equating report views or completed action-plan boxes with success.
   Topics: action-taking, leadership, methods. Evidence: `p14`, "Instead of focusing on".
