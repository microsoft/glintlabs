## Deck — Understanding employee reactions to AI

`deck-understanding-employee-reactions-to-ai` | catalog | reviewed

**Priority:** Original vault; retained tier `high`.

**Source:** https://adoption.microsoft.com/files/viva/Microsoft-Viva_Think-like-a-People-Scientist_Mar2025.pdf

Complete 33-page public March 2025 webinar deck. Uses the April 2024 AI Readiness study, with different analysis samples (1,800 overall; 1,389 profile analysis; 1,155 AI users for RIVA). Same study as the readiness eBook and AI Profiles Drop, not independent corroboration.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete current cached public PDF page text reviewed. Public PDF figures visually inspected on pages 9, 10, 11, 17, 24, 28. Other image-only details are not certified. Linked publications, courses, and other companion assets are not newly reviewed unless explicitly stated.

**Study-family links:** ai-readiness-april-2024

1. **Change fatigue and engaged support for AI** (finding) - The deck juxtaposes Gartner's 2022 enterprise-change context with the April 2024 readiness study's association between engagement and eagerness to support AI. These are separate sources and dates, not a single new 2025 experiment.
   Topics: change-management, engagement, methods. Evidence: `p5`, "Employees are losing patience".
2. **Bring-your-own AI precedes formal sponsorship** (finding) - The presented study reports 67% using at least some tools not supplied by their organization; the access categories distinguish all, most, some, and no tools provided. AI use is not equivalent to fully sponsored access.
   Topics: ai-adoption, ai-governance. Evidence: `p9`, "Are the gen AI tools".
3. **Executive optimism is not evidence of realized returns** (finding) - The deck reports that 78% of executives see generative AI as both critical to organizational success and worth investment. These are expectations that may support sponsorship, not measured financial returns.
   Topics: leadership, ai-strategy, ai-value. Evidence: `p10`, "worth the investment".
4. **Burnout risk co-occurs with reduced change capacity** (finding) - The inspected graph links more burnout risk factors with more stress/strain and lower perceived ability to keep up. Among those with three or more risk factors, ability is 52% while both stress and strain are 70%; this is an association, not a clinical diagnostic or AI effect.
   Topics: wellbeing, change-management. Evidence: `p11`, "No burnout risk factors".
5. **Profiles are data-derived, sample-specific groupings** (method) - Latent profile analysis combines engagement, prior change experience, and AI sentiment; change experience spans vision, communication, and competence. A five-profile solution was selected by model fit in 1,389 respondents. Data-derived groupings do not remove all modeling assumptions or certify permanent employee types.
   Topics: methods, ai-adoption, employee-listening. Evidence: `p17`, "A five-profile solution was selected".
6. **Five profiles distinguish engagement, change, and AI sentiment** (framework) - The study labels Multipliers, Advocates, Persuadables, AI Skeptics, and Change Pessimists. AI Skeptics can be engaged and positive about prior change while cautious about AI, so skepticism is not synonymous with disengagement.
   Topics: ai-adoption, engagement, change-management. Evidence: `p17`, "AI Transformation Profiles".
7. **AI-use frequency differs across profiles** (finding) - The inspected chart and chi-square analysis show profile membership associated with use frequency in the 1,389-person analysis. Multipliers report more daily use, whereas skeptics and pessimists more often report never using AI; no causal profile effect is established.
   Topics: ai-adoption, methods. Evidence: `p24`, "AI Usage Frequency by AI Transformation Profile".
8. **Give each profile a constructive contribution** (recommendation) - Invite enthusiastic employees as SMEs and champions, advocates to share practical use cases, persuadables to test realistic visions, skeptics to raise risks, and change pessimists to identify lessons from failed changes. Concern is treated as useful input rather than a defect.
   Topics: change-management, inclusion, ai-governance. Evidence: `p25`, "Encourage this profile".
9. **Tailor support to different barriers** (recommendation) - Provide experimentation opportunities to Multipliers, direction and change buddies to Advocates, feedback outlets and a concrete vision to Persuadables, benefits/safeguards information to skeptics, and time, resources, and substantial change support to pessimists.
   Topics: change-management, learning-enablement, ai-adoption. Evidence: `p26`, "Support this profile".
10. **Perceived individual value differs among AI-using profiles** (finding) - Among 1,155 AI users, the inspected six-outcome RIVA chart and logistic regressions show differences in perceived task speed, complexity, productivity, quality, stress, and decisions by profile. These are self-reported benefits, not audited organizational ROI or independent corroboration of the same readiness study.
   Topics: ai-value, methods. Evidence: `p28`, "n = 1155".
11. **Meet people where they are without fixed labels** (recommendation) - The deck's closing guidance says profile membership is not static, recommends nonjudgmental support, and focuses attention on employee-experience moments and sound change management.
   Topics: change-management, inclusion. Evidence: `p31`, "without judgement".
12. **Overall, profile, and AI-user samples differ** (method) - The deck describes a global full-time sample from organizations with more than 1,000 employees, heavily weighted toward North America. Overall research, profile modeling, and RIVA charts use different denominators, so percentages should not be mixed as if drawn from identical subsets.
   Topics: methods. Evidence: `p8`, "Study Sample".
