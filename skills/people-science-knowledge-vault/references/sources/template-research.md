## How Microsoft People Science drives Viva Glint programs

`template-research` | catalog | reviewed

**Priority:** Original vault; retained tier `low`.

**Source:** https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/viva/glint/setup/template-research

Published explanation of template construction, drawing on 350M survey responses. Reported relationships are associations; a templated survey is not automatically composed only of validated items.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete freshly retrieved public Microsoft Learn article text reviewed, including substantive tables. Sign-in notices and page chrome excluded as evidence. Linked pages, courses, downloads, recordings and image-only details were not reviewed; no refreshed comprehensive figure or media review is claimed.

1. **Interdisciplinary People Science** (documentation) - People Science combines industrial-organizational and occupational psychology, organizational development, data science, product management and design in a research-backed, people-centric approach.
   Topics: people-science. Evidence: `L19`, "People Science is a research-backed, people-centric approach to the study of happiness and success at work. It combines industrial-organizational psyc".
2. **Driver research and template construction** (documentation) - The page reports analysis of over 350 million employee survey responses to identify experiences predictive of engagement, defined here as happiness at work and willingness to recommend. Driver themes informed templates and the People Success Elements; this is not evidence that a template intervention causes improvement.
   Topics: people-science, methods, survey-design. Evidence: `L21`, "To date, Microsoft People Science analyzed over 350 million employee survey responses to determine the employee experiences that were most predictive ".
3. **Six needs with company-specific expression** (documentation) - The six People Success Elements summarize core needs and expectations, but the experiences driving engagement differ across companies.
   Topics: people-science, engagement. Evidence: `L23`, "The experiences that drive engagement look different from one company to another. Microsoft People Science research identified six core elements which".
4. **Purpose and employer recommendation** (documentation) - The page reports employees with a sense of purpose are 120% more likely to recommend their company as a great workplace. It provides no study-level sample or adjustment details here; treat this as a reported association.
   Topics: meaningful-work, engagement. Evidence: `L27`, "Employees feeling a sense of purpose are 120% more likely to recommend their company as a great place to work.".
5. **Role clarity and reported burnout** (documentation) - In the cited 2021 comparison, 54% of employees lacking role clarity reported burnout versus 38% with clarity. The page does not establish causality or a current organizational baseline.
   Topics: role-clarity, wellbeing. Evidence: `L29`, "54% of employees lacking role clarity reported burnout in 2021, compared with 38% of employees with role clarity.".
6. **Growth and staying intentions** (documentation) - Employees seeing growth opportunities are reported as three times more likely to say they plan to remain at the company in two years. This concerns stated intentions, not verified future retention.
   Topics: growth-development, retention. Evidence: `L31`, "Employees who see opportunities to grow are three times more likely to say they plan to be working at the company in two years.".
7. **Empowerment and happiness** (documentation) - Empowered employees are reported as 2.4 times more likely to be happy at work. The documentation gives a relationship, not an estimated effect of an empowerment intervention.
   Topics: empowerment, engagement. Evidence: `L33`, "Employees empowered at work are 2.4 times more likely to be happy at work.".
8. **Belonging and engagement** (documentation) - Employees with strong belonging are reported as six times more likely to be engaged; study design and adjustment details are not supplied in this page.
   Topics: connection, inclusion, engagement. Evidence: `L35`, "Employees with a strong sense of belonging are six times more likely to be engaged.".
