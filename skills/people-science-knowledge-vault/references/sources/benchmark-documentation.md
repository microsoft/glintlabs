## People Science Explained - Benchmark Documentation

`benchmark-documentation` | catalog | reviewed

**Priority:** Original vault; retained tier `top5`.

**Source:** https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/s/gxcuf89792/attachments/gxcuf89792/Viva_Glint_Blog/131/2/PSE_2025_Benchmark%20Documentation_Public.pdf

Full 24-page public 2025 benchmark guide. Numerical score examples illustrate reporting; they are not actual customer results.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete extracted text of the public 24-page PDF. Linked courses and other resources are separate assets, not automatically reviewed. No claim of complete linked-recording coverage.

1. **Benchmarks as external context** (framework) - Norms show relative standing across organizations but are not prescriptions for what each organization should achieve.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-interpretation. Evidence: `p9`, "perspective, not prescriptions".
2. **Standard items and comparability** (recommendation) - Unchanged standard-question wording supports comparison; strategy should still determine which questions are asked.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-design. Evidence: `p5`, "exactly as written".
3. **Selecting benchmark suites** (documentation) - Global, high-performing, industry, geographic, lifecycle, and specialized suites differ; high-performing values are thresholds rather than averages.
   Topics: benchmarks. Evidence: `p6`, "threshold score".
4. **External, internal, and trend lenses** (framework) - External norms orient, internal groups reveal organizational variation, and historical comparisons track a team's own trajectory.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-interpretation. Evidence: `p11`, "Historical".
5. **Broad norms and geographic context** (recommendation) - Broad benchmarks are generally more robust; geographic comparisons can help contextualize cultural response patterns.
   Topics: benchmarks, inclusion. Evidence: `p13`, "cultural/regional".
6. **Behavior goals rather than score targets** (recommendation) - Prioritize business-relevant incremental behaviors instead of chasing arbitrary external score targets.
   Topics: action-taking, benchmarks. Evidence: `p15`, "Avoid setting targets".
7. **Three-stage score aggregation** (method) - The process aggregates item scores within cycles, across cycles per customer, then across customers.
   Topics: benchmarks, methods. Evidence: `p17`, "three stages".
8. **Usage thresholds and representativeness** (method) - Customer-only data and sufficient item usage support coverage; customer weighting avoids simple domination by respondent count.
   Topics: benchmarks, methods. Evidence: `p18`, "each customer is weighted".
9. **Recency and refresh cadence** (documentation) - Rolling annual windows balance stability and recency; global/high-performing suites refresh more often than most other suites.
   Topics: benchmarks, methods. Evidence: `p20`, "Rolling 12-Month".
10. **Report controls and opt-in** (documentation) - Admins enable external comparisons and set defaults; users can switch lenses and inspect heatmaps or exports.
   Topics: benchmarks, glint-admin. Evidence: `p23`, "must opt".
