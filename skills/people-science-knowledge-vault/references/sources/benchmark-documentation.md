## People Science Explained - Benchmark Documentation

`benchmark-documentation` | catalog | reviewed

**Priority:** Original vault; retained tier `top5`.

**Source:** https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/s/gxcuf89792/attachments/gxcuf89792/Viva_Glint_Blog/131/2/PSE_2025_Benchmark%20Documentation_Public.pdf

Complete 24-page public 2025 benchmark guide. Product/reporting descriptions are historical guidance, not a present-day feature check. Numeric score examples are illustrative, not customer results. Customer aggregates are not a representative probability sample of all employers. Norm gaps and change scores alone do not establish root causes or intervention effects.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete current cached public PDF page text reviewed. Embedded figures were not re-reviewed; claims are restricted to readable prose/page text, not image-only details. Linked publications, courses, and other companion assets are not newly reviewed unless explicitly stated.

1. **External norms orient interpretation rather than prescribe success** (framework) - Benchmarks aggregate anonymous cross-company scores to contextualize relative strengths and weaknesses; they are reference points, not definitions of what a particular organization must achieve.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-interpretation. Evidence: `p3`, "Benchmarks are aggregated external reference".
2. **Preserve exact standard-item wording for comparability** (recommendation) - Use standard questions as written when external comparison is needed, because wording changes can affect results. Choose content for strategic relevance rather than maximizing the number of benchmarked items.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-design. Evidence: `p5`, "questions exactly as written".
3. **Percentile benchmarks are item-level entry thresholds** (documentation) - High-performing top-10% or top-25% norms identify the threshold score for that item, not the mean of a stable group of top companies across every question.
   Topics: benchmarks, methods. Evidence: `p6`, "threshold score to be in the top tier".
4. **Suite availability follows population and data sufficiency** (documentation) - Global, industry, country/region, lifecycle, and specialized suites answer different population questions. Lifecycle items are included where data suffices, and specialty suites are available and renewed only with sufficient data.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-design. Evidence: `p6`, "Only available and renewed if data is sufficient".
5. **Industry labels can conceal larger within-industry differences** (recommendation) - The guide cautions that engagement often varies more within industries than between them, industry labels miss organizational diversity, and talent markets cross industries. A narrower sector norm is not automatically more applicable.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-interpretation, methods. Evidence: `p6`, "more variation within industries than".
6. **Function-specific and custom cuts are not supported** (documentation) - Inconsistent definitions of functions make comparable functional benchmarks impractical. The guide also declines custom cuts because narrow samples are less robust, costly, and often less useful than broad comparisons.
   Topics: benchmarks, methods. Evidence: `p6`, "does not offer function-".
7. **Triangulate external, internal, and historical comparisons** (framework) - External norms supply market context, internal comparisons reveal relative team patterns, and historical scores track an organization's own progress. Use the three together rather than treating one lens as a complete explanation or causal evaluation.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-interpretation. Evidence: `p11`, "richest insights come from seeing".
8. **Broad norms usually provide a stronger reference** (recommendation) - Prefer global or high-performing norms for broad talent markets and complex organizations. Narrow cuts need a strategic reason and may rest on fewer companies; specificity alone is not quality.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-interpretation. Evidence: `p13`, "a broader".
9. **Geographic response tendencies warrant contextual checks** (recommendation) - Country or regional norms can contextualize differences potentially influenced by response culture. Compare relevant populations and avoid inferring that a global gap necessarily signals a local problem; local norms supplement rather than replace broader context.
   Topics: benchmarks, inclusion, survey-interpretation. Evidence: `p13`, "cultural response tendencies".
10. **Custom questions can be useful without external norms** (recommendation) - Survey strategically important unique topics even when no external benchmark exists; interpret them using internal trends, group comparisons, and qualitative follow-up instead of omitting them.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-design, survey-interpretation. Evidence: `p14`, "that shouldn’t deter you from asking".
11. **Explain benchmark meaning and confidentiality to stakeholders** (recommendation) - Describe benchmarks as anonymous aggregates, calibrate unrealistic executive expectations, and keep employee experience and business outcomes as the objective rather than chasing a reference score.
   Topics: benchmarks, privacy-confidentiality, leadership. Evidence: `p14`, "not a prescriptive target".
12. **Investigate gaps and preserve strengths before choosing action** (recommendation) - A large gap can reflect composition, a temporary situation, a strategic trade-off, or a genuine opportunity. Explore why, then balance closing relevant gaps with sustaining and sharing strengths; the examples do not identify a local root cause.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-interpretation, action-taking. Evidence: `p15`, "Translate benchmark gaps into action thoughtfully".
13. **Set directional behavior improvements rather than score targets** (recommendation) - Prioritize incremental, business-linked behavior changes rather than benchmark-based numeric targets that may be unachievable or encourage unhealthy score-chasing.
   Topics: benchmarks, action-taking. Evidence: `p15`, "Avoid setting targets".
14. **Aggregation gives each customer an item score** (method) - The described pipeline aggregates at survey-cycle level, averages the customer's surveys into one score per item, then averages item scores across customers. It is not a pooled average giving every employee response equal weight.
   Topics: benchmarks, methods. Evidence: `p17`, "aggregated in three stages".
15. **Representativeness concerns organizations rather than raw response totals** (method) - The guide emphasizes number, diversity, and representativeness of participating organizations; its weighting description says organizations in a size category are weighted alike. A headline respondent total alone does not describe the effective item-level evidence base.
   Topics: benchmarks, methods. Evidence: `p17`, "number, diversity,".
16. **Item-usage audits determine coverage** (method) - Twice-yearly usage audits determine which questions have sufficient data; the guide reports more than 175 benchmarked items. It does not publish an item-specific universal minimum in this text.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-design, methods. Evidence: `p18`, "item usage audit twice".
17. **Customer-only norms have a new-content seeding exception** (method) - Standard norms have used customer scores since early 2021. The guide notes a rare exception for seeding brand-new content with panel data, replaced when sufficient client data becomes available.
   Topics: benchmarks, methods. Evidence: `p18`, "initial seeding of brand-new content".
18. **Rolling windows balance stability and recency** (documentation) - The guide uses a rolling 12-month window and describes semiannual global/top-percentile updates with most industry/country updates annual. This is refresh methodology, not proof that successive versions form an unchanged longitudinal sample.
   Topics: benchmarks, methods. Evidence: `p20`, "Rolling 12-Month Data Window".
19. **Report interpretation depends on the active comparator and vintage** (documentation) - Users may switch comparison groups; suite names and years identify the external norm, while deltas, detailed values, heatmaps, and exports reflect the selected reference. The displayed score examples are illustrations, not customer findings.
   Topics: benchmarks, survey-interpretation, glint-admin. Evidence: `p21`, "Report Settings".
20. **External benchmark access requires organizational opt-in** (documentation) - The guide links administrator consent for anonymous external processing with benchmark visibility. Without opt-in, users see internal comparators; admins should select a sensible default and orient managers to switching views.
   Topics: benchmarks, privacy-confidentiality, glint-admin. Evidence: `p23`, "must opt-in to use external benchmarks".
