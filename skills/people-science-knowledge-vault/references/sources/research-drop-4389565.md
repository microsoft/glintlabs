## Research Drop: Investing in HR & IT Collaboration to Drive Successful AI Transformation

`research-drop-4389565` | research-drop | reviewed

**Priority:** New catalog; before relevant original-vault resources.

**Source:** https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftvivablog/research-drop-investing-in-hr--it-collaboration-to-drive-successful-ai-transform/4389565

March 2025 article analyzing 413 global HR and IT employees from the April 2024 AI-readiness panel. Shared evidence family with the HR/IT webinar/deck and readiness eBook, not an independent replication. Sponsorship, training confidence, use, and RIVA are concurrently reported associations; RIVA is perceived individual benefit, not audited financial return. External organizational statistics and the employee-self-service illustration are separate from panel findings.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete freshly retrieved public article body read, including substantive published method/item appendices where present. Figures, chart pixels, linked assets, audio, private decks, and notes were not reviewed in this pass. Current themes are grounded in body prose, not chart alt text. Prior figure review is historical only and is recorded in the audit; it is not refreshed coverage.

**Study-family links:** ai-readiness-april-2024

1. **HR and IT contribute complementary expertise** (framework) - IT contributes technical systems and orchestration, while HR contributes skills, talent, and employee-change expertise. Employee self-service is an illustrative way to reduce transactional HR work, not an evaluated product or financial-ROI finding in this panel.
   Topics: hr-it, change-management. Evidence: `L6`, "IT brings the tech; HR brings the people".
2. **Both functions are positive, with IT further ahead** (finding) - The sample reported optimism that AI improves work at 73% for HR and 82% for IT; anticipated revenue/financial benefit was also lower in HR. These differences describe perceptions and functional context, not competence or verified financial performance.
   Topics: hr-it, ai-adoption, ai-value. Evidence: `L7`, "73% of HR employees and 82% of IT employees".
3. **Security and skilling are shared collaboration goals** (recommendation) - Combine HR's employee-data and learning expertise with IT's security and technical guidance. The article recommends common priorities and complementary roles rather than treating the partnership as a transfer of all responsibility to one function.
   Topics: hr-it, ai-governance, ai-skills. Evidence: `L11`, "a shared priority for both groups is data security".
4. **Sponsored access is unequal across HR and IT** (finding) - All or most AI tools were organization-sponsored for 72% of IT and 59% of HR respondents; weekly-or-more use was also lower in HR. Unequal sponsorship can complicate centralized training and measurement, but these data do not establish its cause.
   Topics: hr-it, ai-adoption, inclusion. Evidence: `L14`, "72% of IT employees".
5. **Higher sponsorship accompanies higher perceived value** (finding) - In both functions, employees with high sponsored access reported more RIVA benefits. The author notes tools may arrive with leadership support, training, and scenario resources, so the association does not isolate a tool-access effect.
   Topics: ai-value, ai-adoption, hr-it, methods. Evidence: `L17`, "Organization-sponsored tools likely come with leadership support".
6. **Task speed is easier to recognize than broader benefits** (finding) - Task-speed benefits appeared even among low-access respondents, whereas decision quality and stress reduction were harder to realize. Transformation should consider deeper role-relevant use cases rather than equating fast task completion with full organizational return.
   Topics: ai-value, work-design. Evidence: `L18`, "the more subtle benefits are the hardest to achieve".
7. **Make cross-functional training and resourcing equitable** (recommendation) - Broad training agreement masked weaker strong agreement, and the article cites HR resource constraints. Provide HR with tools, time, staffing, and AI/data development so it can partner strategically rather than only handle transactional uses.
   Topics: hr-it, learning-enablement, ai-skills. Evidence: `L23`, "a lack of resources (e.g., time, money, staff)".
8. **Build experimentation networks across functional silos** (recommendation) - Use common language, forums, prompt/use-case sharing, and a joint HR/IT taskforce to spread learning beyond teams with early access. These are proposed practices; the panel does not test their implementation effects.
   Topics: hr-it, connection, learning-enablement. Evidence: `L26`, "Design inclusive, common languages".
9. **Use employee voice alongside technology behavior** (recommendation) - HR can involve employees in identifying automation opportunities and combine sentiment with technology-use measures alongside IT. This broadens readiness, adoption, and impact assessment without treating either tool activity or perceived value as audited ROI.
   Topics: hr-it, employee-listening, ai-value. Evidence: `L28`, "employee technology behaviors and employee sentiment feedback".
