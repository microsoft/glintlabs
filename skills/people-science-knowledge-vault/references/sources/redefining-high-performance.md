## Redefining High Performance in the New Era of Work

`redefining-high-performance` | catalog | reviewed

**Priority:** Original vault; retained tier `curated`.

**Source:** https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/final/en-us/microsoft-brand/documents/Microsoft-HPO-Guide-Oct-2023.pdf

Complete 28-page October 2023 public guide recovered from Microsoft's official CDN. Main online panel: 1,100 leaders, September 6–14, 2023, nine markets, organizations with 1,000+ employees. HPO status is leaders' self-report (15%), unlike 17% in the later AI-readiness study. Separate analyses cover more than 3M responses at 226 public companies and learning/growth associations at 246 customers. Leader perceptions, organizational comparisons, customer examples, and proposed AI applications are not causal intervention evidence.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete current cached public PDF page text reviewed. Embedded figures were not re-reviewed; claims are restricted to readable prose/page text, not image-only details. Linked publications, courses, and other companion assets are not newly reviewed unless explicitly stated.

**Study-family links:** high-performance-september-2023

1. **Performance is broader than financial results** (framework) - Leaders' performance indicators include productive teams, innovation, growth, collaboration, and resilient culture as well as profits. The guide stresses that an organization's definition depends on its own goals, industry, work setting, and size.
   Topics: people-science, leadership, engagement. Evidence: `p14`, "Each organization will have its own definition".
2. **HPO classification is based on leaders' reported performance** (method) - The study's HPOs are the 15% reporting that their organizations always deliver on at least six of ten indicators. It is a self-reported study classification, not an independently verified designation or the 17% prevalence in the later readiness study.
   Topics: methods, people-science. Evidence: `p6`, "6 of 10 performance".
3. **Engagement and market performance come from a separate analysis** (finding) - The guide cites anonymized feedback from more than three million employees at 226 public companies and prior-year stock performance. This is separate from the 1,100-leader survey and does not show engagement interventions caused stock outperformance.
   Topics: engagement, methods. Evidence: `p5`, "226 publicly listed companies".
4. **Three mutually reinforcing pillars organize high performance** (framework) - The framework combines engaged employees, productive teams, and resilient business. Its nine supporting capabilities cover managers, wellbeing, focus, collaboration, processes, enabling tools, agility, skill development, and innovation.
   Topics: people-science, engagement, work-design. Evidence: `p7`, "three pillars".
5. **Effective managers are important and difficult to enable** (finding) - Leaders ranked managers who empower and develop teams as the most important capability and the hardest to master. The guide treats manager capability and hybrid-work trust as organizational priorities, not just individual motivation issues.
   Topics: manager-effectiveness, empowerment, leadership. Evidence: `p9`, "single most".
6. **Team productivity requires enabling processes and tools** (finding) - HPO leaders more often identified collaboration as a performance indicator; efficient processes and empowering data/tools support teamwork. Leaders also ranked inefficient work culture as a leading barrier, so productivity is not just individual output.
   Topics: work-design, connection, empowerment. Evidence: `p10`, "Creating efficient processes".
7. **Resilience combines agility, skills, and innovation** (framework) - The guide frames resilience as adapting priorities, enabling new responsibilities through upskilling/reskilling, and supporting creativity and experimentation, rather than merely enduring adversity.
   Topics: work-design, growth-development, leadership. Evidence: `p10`, "agility to change".
8. **Understand skill gaps before deciding development investments** (finding) - Leaders reported difficulty identifying existing workforce skills, constraining reskilling and talent mobility. The guide recommends making skill visibility and development priorities explicit.
   Topics: growth-development, learning-enablement, work-design. Evidence: `p11`, "understanding the existing skill sets".
9. **Learning opportunity is associated with engagement and intended staying** (finding) - A separately cited 246-customer analysis links favorable learning/growth responses with happiness, perceived career support, and intended two-year retention. These are proportional survey associations, not observed exit-rate multipliers or causal training effects.
   Topics: growth-development, engagement, retention, methods. Evidence: `p11`, "expect they will".
10. **Prioritize capabilities by importance and difficulty** (framework) - The guide offers transformation for high-importance/high-difficulty capabilities, amplification for important established strengths, and optimization for foundational processes/data/tools. Match investment and technology to the relevant challenge rather than treating every capability alike.
   Topics: leadership, action-taking, people-science. Evidence: `p17`, "Transformation".
11. **AI is proposed as an accelerant to people-centric barriers** (hypothesis) - Leaders saw potential for AI to reduce tedious tasks, inefficient work culture, burnout/disengagement, skill gaps, and manager burden. These are expectations about possible benefits, not a trial demonstrating that AI removes the barriers.
   Topics: ai-value, ai-strategy, wellbeing. Evidence: `p19`, "potential to address".
12. **AI investments often begin with foundations** (finding) - The survey reports AI investment centered on infrastructure, workforce skills, and adoption business objectives. HPO leaders more often reported employee-experience use cases; this is a descriptive investment pattern, not a prescribed number of investments.
   Topics: ai-adoption, ai-strategy, ai-skills. Evidence: `p20`, "data infrastructure".
13. **Deep collaboration includes disagreement and shared investment** (finding) - Leaders more often described friendly relationship-building than co-deciding investment, sponsoring others, and politely debating disagreement. HPO leaders reported more difficult forms of collaboration; the comparison does not establish a causal collaboration treatment.
   Topics: connection, leadership, feedback. Evidence: `p22`, "co-deciding on investments".
14. **HR and IT should co-decide on people-enabling technology** (recommendation) - HR and IT more often worked within their own functions than together on technology investment. The guide recommends using shared priorities such as expectations, communication, and skills as a basis for deeper co-sponsored change.
   Topics: hr-it, leadership, change-management. Evidence: `p23`, "co-deciding on investments".
15. **Digital transformation roles can bridge people and technology** (framework) - Digital Experience/Transformation leaders are described as translators and mediators between HR expertise and IT delivery, helping avoid technically successful products that overlook human requirements.
   Topics: hr-it, work-design. Evidence: `p23`, "both mediator".
16. **Use aggregate work patterns to inform concrete work redesign** (case-study) - The published PepsiCo example combines engagement and collaboration analytics to investigate hybrid work and encourage fewer, shorter meetings, while developing shared accountability. It is a reported customer case without controlled effect estimates.
   Topics: work-design, employee-listening, wellbeing. Evidence: `p25`, "The Gift of Time".
17. **Assess with active and passive evidence before acting** (recommendation) - Establish a baseline using employee sentiment and workplace patterns such as meetings, focus, and manager contact. Look for useful relationships and opportunities without treating passive signals as proof of causes of low engagement.
   Topics: employee-listening, methods, action-taking. Evidence: `p26`, "holistic understanding".
18. **Measure outcomes rather than assume technology creates value** (recommendation) - Choose data-informed actions, then examine relevant productivity, engagement, business, and cost outcomes over time. The assess–act–measure loop is guidance, not proof that a simple before/after comparison identifies causal impact.
   Topics: action-taking, ai-value, methods. Evidence: `p26`, "Measure Impact".
19. **Multiple studies and historical scope limit inference** (method) - The main online panel surveyed 1,100 leaders September 6–14, 2023 across nine markets and organizations with 1,000+ employees. The guide separately cites public-company engagement data, learning/growth analysis, and WTI research; do not combine those denominators or call them independent validations of one intervention.
   Topics: methods. Evidence: `p28`, "September 6, 2023 and September 14, 2023".
