## The state of AI change readiness

`ai-change-readiness` | catalog | reviewed

**Priority:** Original vault; retained tier `top5`.

**Source:** https://adoption.microsoft.com/files/viva/The-state-of-AI-change-readiness-eBook.pdf

All 24 public PDF pages text-reviewed. April 2024 online panel: 1,800 full-time respondents across nine markets, organizations with 1,000+ employees; 61% North America. Readiness/RIVA regression uses n=1,443 AI users; prior-change regression and profiles use n=1,389. Self-report observational findings, not audited financial ROI or causal effects. HPO status is respondent-rated (17% of this study), unlike 15% in the earlier HPO guide. Shared lineage with the profiles/HR-IT decks and multiple 2024–2025 Drops is preserved.

**Reviewed asset scope:** Complete current cached public PDF page text reviewed. Embedded figures were not re-reviewed; claims are restricted to readable prose/page text, not image-only details. Linked publications, courses, and other companion assets are not newly reviewed unless explicitly stated.

**Study-family links:** ai-readiness-april-2024

1. **Employee AI use runs ahead of organizational sponsorship** (finding) - The April 2024 study reports 80% using AI at work, with 67% of users using at least some tools not supplied by their organization. These sample estimates differ from the separately cited Work Trend Index's 75% knowledge-worker estimate.
   Topics: ai-adoption, ai-governance, methods. Evidence: `p5`, "67% of those using AI".
2. **Executive support coexists with expected transformation effort** (finding) - Most executives saw AI as critical and worth investment, while about two-thirds expected more time and effort than other technology changes. The guide recommends channeling existing enthusiasm instead of assuming sponsorship alone makes transformation easy.
   Topics: leadership, ai-strategy, change-management. Evidence: `p5`, "time and effort than other".
3. **Burnout and change saturation constrain perceived capacity** (finding) - More self-reported burnout risk factors co-occurred with greater stress/strain and lower confidence in keeping up. Account for other organizational changes and create time for new habits rather than treating AI as only a technical rollout.
   Topics: wellbeing, change-management. Evidence: `p7`, "three or more burnout risk factors".
4. **AI-use frequency and employee experience are correlated** (finding) - More frequent users reported more favorable engagement, recommendation, retention intentions, and individual/team productivity. The source explicitly describes the pattern as correlational; selection, prior engagement, and other factors prevent causal attribution.
   Topics: engagement, retention, ai-adoption, methods. Evidence: `p8`, "correlational in nature".
5. **RIVA captures perceived individual benefits beyond speed** (finding) - Realized Individual Value of AI includes faster work, productivity, simpler tasks, better quality, better decisions, and less work stress. Engaged AI users report higher value, even controlling frequent use; this is experience evidence rather than audited organizational returns.
   Topics: ai-value, engagement, methods. Evidence: `p9`, "Realized Individual Value of AI".
6. **A reciprocal use-value cycle is a hypothesis** (hypothesis) - The authors hypothesize that experienced benefits encourage continued AI use, which may create a virtuous cycle. Cross-sectional associations do not demonstrate the proposed temporal feedback mechanism.
   Topics: ai-value, ai-adoption, methods. Evidence: `p9`, "We hypothesize".
7. **Five facets define employee AI readiness** (framework) - Readiness comprises understanding integration opportunities, motivation, skills, opportunity to integrate, and seeing value. It is broader than access, tool frequency, or willingness alone.
   Topics: ai-adoption, ai-skills, people-science. Evidence: `p10`, "What does an AI ready employee look like?".
8. **Readiness model explains variance rather than causal value creation** (method) - For 1,443 AI users, a regression controlling job level and current use frequency links readiness with RIVA and reports up to 62% explained variance. This is not a 62% causal improvement, guaranteed ROI, or unique contribution of a training program.
   Topics: methods, ai-value, ai-adoption. Evidence: `p10`, "n = 1443".
9. **Support readiness through examples, skills, time, and feedback** (recommendation) - Provide role-specific examples, practical peer learning, protected experimentation time, feedback on value, and success stories that include overcoming barriers. These are proposed implementation supports, not separately tested causal levers.
   Topics: learning-enablement, ai-skills, feedback. Evidence: `p10`, "Tips for supporting AI readiness".
10. **Past change experience is associated with current AI readiness** (method) - A regression of 1,389 respondents controlling job level reports up to 43% of AI-readiness variance associated with previous change experience. The analysis motivates strong change habits without proving that improving past experience will cause a specified readiness gain.
   Topics: methods, change-management, ai-adoption. Evidence: `p11`, "n = 1389".
11. **Leaders and contributors experience change differently** (finding) - Leaders report stronger communication, learning opportunities, and inclusion than individual contributors. Gather employee feedback before, during, and after change, and avoid using leaders' positive experiences as evidence that frontline support is adequate.
   Topics: leadership, inclusion, employee-listening, change-management. Evidence: `p11`, "risk of assumed alignment".
12. **HPO status is a respondent-rated classification** (method) - The study classifies the 17% who reported always delivering on at least six of ten performance indicators as HPO respondents. This is not an objective audit, nor the same 15% classification prevalence reported in the 2023 high-performance guide.
   Topics: methods, people-science. Evidence: `p4`, "at least 6/10".
13. **HPO respondents describe more people-centric change** (finding) - Respondents at HPOs more often report respect, care, involvement in decisions, feedback receptiveness, and positive change experiences. The classification and outcomes share respondent perceptions, limiting causal interpretation.
   Topics: change-management, inclusion, leadership. Evidence: `p13`, "felt included in decisions".
14. **Connect individual AI benefit with organizational purpose** (finding) - Many contributors see value for their own tasks without seeing AI as critical to organizational success; the guide recommends cascading a clear vision linking personal benefits to shared goals. HPO respondents report a smaller gap.
   Topics: ai-strategy, meaningful-work, leadership. Evidence: `p14`, "micro-value".
15. **Sponsored access and experimentation travel together** (finding) - HPO respondents report more employer-provided tools, more integration opportunities, and more use. The guide proposes sponsored experimentation and sharing use cases; group differences alone do not prove an access intervention caused greater value.
   Topics: ai-adoption, inclusion, ai-value. Evidence: `p15`, "access to organization-".
16. **Two-way communication and visible feedback action** (recommendation) - Use channels that capture employee responses, gather input early and often, and route feedback to people able to adjust implementation. Pair milestone recognition with empathy and clarity about role changes and unknowns.
   Topics: employee-listening, feedback, change-management. Evidence: `p16`, "transparent two-way".
17. **Match experimentation structure to role and investment stage** (recommendation) - Combine informal general exploration with organized role-specific testing as investments deepen. Methods include communities, prompt libraries, academies, surveys, hackathons, and workflow mapping; no single experiment format fits every function.
   Topics: ai-skills, learning-enablement, work-design. Evidence: `p17`, "Organized and specific testing".
18. **Use small, flexible, co-created change cycles** (recommendation) - Break the vision into tangible steps, adapt it to feedback and shifting context, and co-build with employees. The guidance favors iterative role-specific rollouts rather than waiting indefinitely for a final AI end-state.
   Topics: change-management, action-taking. Evidence: `p18`, "Co-build with your".
19. **Champion communities extend practical peer support** (case-study) - Microsoft's case describes nominated and volunteer champions adapting resources for colleagues, owning local experiments, sharing lessons, and using community platforms. It illustrates an approach, not a proven scalable treatment effect.
   Topics: learning-enablement, ai-skills, connection. Evidence: `p19`, "Building an AI Champion Community".
20. **Data-derived profiles call for differentiated, inclusive support** (framework) - A latent-profile analysis of 1,389 respondents combines engagement, past change, and AI sentiment into Multipliers, Advocates, Persuadables, AI Skeptics, and Change Pessimists. Invite all profiles into experimentation, matching opportunities, direction, feedback, safeguards, and support to needs rather than excluding skeptics.
   Topics: methods, ai-adoption, inclusion, change-management. Evidence: `p20`, "AI transformation profiles".
21. **Use is not endorsement: surface security, reliance, and job concerns** (finding) - AI users can still be skeptical. The study highlights personal/organizational data protection, unvalidated automatic decisions, and job-loss/deskilling concerns; leaders should listen, explain safeguards, and support reskilling and upskilling.
   Topics: ai-governance, employee-listening, ai-skills. Evidence: `p21`, "AI usealone does not indicate full support".
22. **HR and IT should share responsibility for responsible transformation** (recommendation) - The closing principles advocate cross-disciplinary collaboration to protect organizational and personal data and identify useful applications, combining risk management with opportunity discovery.
   Topics: hr-it, ai-governance, ai-strategy. Evidence: `p22`, "IT and HR can work together".
23. **Panel design and reused samples limit independent corroboration** (method) - The online panel collected 1,800 full-time responses across nine markets on March 29–April 9, 2024, in organizations with 1,000+ employees. The report reuses this study in multiple analyses and later decks/Drops; these publications are not separate replications.
   Topics: methods. Evidence: `p24`, "March 29, 2024 and April 9, 2024".

## Shared research context
