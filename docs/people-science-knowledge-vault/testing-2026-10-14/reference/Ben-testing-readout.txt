FINDINGS TO ADDRESS
1. Evidence reporting: one source was cited without recorded body retrieval.
   In question 11, exchange 2, GPT said it reviewed two article bodies. The trace
   shows the psychological-safety article returned zero lines, followed by a search
   snippet and an error. Only the Learn page had a recorded readable body. The
   unsupported source was reused in exchanges 5 and 7. The article's claims may
   be accurate, but the stated retrieval provenance does not meet the skill's rule.
   Evidence: retrieval-evidence-issue.txt and question 11's saved events/replies.

2. Formal presentation and internal source labels recur.
   27 of 100 replies use the Cross-source summary heading; 27 include an internal
   priority-label phrase. These are observed wording counts, not quality scores.
   Many practical replies still read like mini research reports. Some later scripts
   are concise and conversational, showing the model can adapt but not consistently.

3. Practical direction sometimes arrives after repeated clarification.
   In question 2, the user states that they are a manager and do not know where to
   start. GPT asks another set of questions before giving a concrete starting plan
   after the next follow-up. Relevant clarification should remain; the proposed edit
   helps the user choose a path instead of requiring them to design the approach.

4. Questionnaire review versus data analysis could be clearer upfront.
   In question 6, the first reply routes own-survey review broadly to analyze-survey.
   Later it correctly says an initial wording/coverage review can use published
   guidance without response data. Preserve that useful distinction from the start.

5. Keep example scripts conditional when results were never supplied.
   Question 10, exchange 7, calls a hypothetical Role fit/Belonging contrast an
   observation in sample manager wording. It may be intended as illustration, but
   should use conditional wording or placeholders so it is not copied as a known fact.
